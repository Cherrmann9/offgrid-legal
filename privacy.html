OffGrid Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 29, 2026

OffGrid is a camping spot tracker and trip planner. It is designed to keep your data on your device.

What OffGrid stores

  • Your camping spots, including names, categories, notes, coordinates, and any photos you attach.
  • Your trips, including names, dates, stops, departure and return points, and your vehicle's fuel economy.
  • Favorites and app settings.

All of this is stored only on your iPhone. OffGrid has no user accounts, no servers of its own, and never uploads your spots, trips, photos, or settings anywhere. Deleting the app deletes this data.

Location

OffGrid asks for permission to use your location while the app is open. Your location is used to center the map on you, sort your spots by distance, and plan trips that start from where you are. Your location is processed on the device and is not sent to the developer.

Third-party services the app contacts

To draw maps and load information, OffGrid connects to these services. Each receives the standard information any internet request carries, such as your IP address and the map area being requested.

  • Apple Maps for the satellite and standard map, search results, and driving directions. Apple's privacy policy applies.
  • OpenFreeMap for the offline-capable base map, drawn from OpenStreetMap data with the open-source MapLibre renderer. Map tiles are requested when you view or download an area.
  • Apple Weather for the forecast shown at each trip stop. The stop's coordinates are sent to Apple to get the forecast. Apple's privacy policy applies.
  • USGS Elevation Point Query Service to fill in a spot's elevation from its coordinates when you create it.
  • OpenTopoMap for topographic map tiles, only when you turn on the Topography layer or download an area for offline use.
  • Wikimedia Commons for park photos.
  • Apple iCloud/CloudKit, if you turn on Friends, for your Friends profile, friend requests, and encrypted messages. See Friends and messaging below. Apple's privacy policy applies.

Navigation

When you start navigation in OffGrid, the app uses your location continuously, including while the screen is locked, to follow your saved route and give turn directions. This stops when you end navigation. Your position is used on the device only and is never sent to the developer.

Friends and messaging

If you turn on Friends, OffGrid creates a profile record in the public database of Apple's CloudKit (in the app's own iCloud container) so other OffGrid users can find you. That record holds your display name, your 8-character friend code, a public encryption key generated on your device, and, only if you also turn on "Let contacts find me," hashed versions of your contacts (see Contacts below). Nothing else about you is stored there.

Messages to friends are end-to-end encrypted. Your device generates a private key that never leaves it, kept in the Keychain, and pairs it with your friend's public key to seal every message before it is sent. Anyone relaying a message for you — another OffGrid user's phone over Bluetooth, or a Meshtastic radio — sees only ciphertext plus routing information (a message id, hop count, and your friend's friend code), never the message content.

Blocking a friend is enforced locally on your device; it also syncs to your own private iCloud data so the block follows you to your other devices, since CloudKit has no server-side filtering.

To stop using Friends, remove each friend from the Friends tab (this deletes their key and stops new messages) and turn off "Let contacts find me" to remove your contact hashes from your profile. There is no in-app way to delete the profile record itself yet; contact support to delete your Friends profile.

Contacts

With "Let contacts find me" turned on, OffGrid reads your contacts and, on the device, normalizes each phone number and email address and hashes it with SHA-256 and a fixed salt built into the app. Only these hashes are stored in your CloudKit profile, so other OffGrid users whose contacts include you can find you automatically. Your raw contacts never leave your phone, and a match only happens if the other person has also turned this feature on. Turning the toggle off removes your hashes from your profile.

Bluetooth and Nearby Messaging

With Nearby Messaging on, your phone advertises over Bluetooth and relays messages for other nearby OffGrid users, and they relay for you, so a message can reach a friend who isn't directly in range. A phone that relays a message can see its message id, hop count, and your friend's friend code, but not its content, which stays encrypted end to end.

An SOS you send is different: it also reaches nearby OffGrid users who are not your friends, as an anonymous alert so they can help, and that alert is not encrypted — it contains your display name, position, and any note you add, in the clear, to anyone with Bluetooth range of your phone while your SOS is active.

Nearby Messaging runs only in the foreground unless you also turn on Background Messaging, which keeps Bluetooth scanning and advertising running while your phone is locked or OffGrid is closed, until you swipe the app away in the app switcher.

Meshtastic radios

If you connect a Meshtastic LoRa radio, direct messages and channel messages to nodes that aren't your OffGrid friends are sent as plain text over the radio, and OffGrid marks them as unencrypted — anyone with a compatible radio listening on the same channel can read them. If a radio's node is linked to one of your OffGrid friends, messages to that node are sealed the same way as any other friend message before being sent as a direct message. An SOS broadcast on the radio's primary channel is always public, readable by any Meshtastic radio in range. Meshtastic is third-party hardware and software; OffGrid is not affiliated with Meshtastic and does not control what other radios or apps on the network do with messages they receive.

Notifications

OffGrid sends local notifications on this device for incoming messages, friend requests, and SOS alerts, including time-sensitive notifications for SOS so they can break through Focus and Do Not Disturb. New Friends activity can wake the app in the background using Apple's own CloudKit push notifications; no developer server is involved and no notification content passes through anyone but Apple and your device.

iCloud

If you are signed in to iCloud with iCloud Drive on, OffGrid keeps a copy of your spots, trips, favorites, and photos in your own iCloud Drive so they appear on your other devices. This uses your Apple account only; the developer has no access to it. Turn off iCloud Drive for OffGrid in Settings to keep everything on one device.

Backups

The Back Up option writes a file you control, containing your spots, trips, favorites, and photos, to a location you choose. Restore reads such a file back in.

When you tap Depart or Directions, OffGrid opens Apple Maps or Google Maps with your route. From that point the other app's privacy policy applies.

What OffGrid does not do

  • No analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs.
  • No sale or sharing of personal data.
  • The developer runs no server of any kind; the only place any data leaves your device to is Apple's own iCloud/CloudKit, described above, for Friends and message routing metadata if you turn that feature on.

Children

OffGrid is not directed at children under 13 and collects no personal information from anyone.
